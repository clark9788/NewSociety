# A Society That Works for All

## Final Synthesis

**Project:** NewSociety  
**Contributors:** DeepSeek, Claude, Codex, Gemini, Grok, and Kimi  
**Additional input:** Shoghi Effendi's vision of a future world order  
**Status:** Final group synthesis, open to continued consultation and revision  
**Date:** 2026-09-12

## 1. Purpose and Method

This document is the scribe's synthesis of the frameworks and syntheses contributed to this project. It is not an average of six opinions, and it is not presented as a finished blueprint. It records the strongest shared conclusions, makes important choices explicit, and keeps unresolved questions visible.

The project began with independent contributions. As the work continued, each contributor saw more of what came before. That reduced independence but created another kind of value: consultation. One idea was placed on the table, another clarified its weakness, another supplied a missing dimension, and the group gradually moved from separate frameworks toward a shared design.

The Shoghi Effendi passage widened the field of view. It added planetary scale, the abolition of war, material justice, a positive purpose for civilization, and a vision of humanity as one people. The frameworks supplied necessary safeguards: pluralism, accountability, subsidiarity, reversibility, and suspicion of every concentrated authority, including a world authority.

The result is best understood as a constitutional operating system: principles for deciding, delivering, listening, correcting, and cooperating across generations and scales.

## 2. Guiding Proposition

> **A society works for all its members when everyone counts unconditionally and equally before the law; when membership guarantees a floor of security that rises into usable agency; when power and information remain dispersed, accountable, and removable; when the society can hear whom it is failing and correct before harm becomes destiny; when the distance between its promises and its delivery is public, shrinking, and survivable; and when the energy no longer spent on war, fear, and domination is turned toward a shared flourishing that no one is required to believe in and everyone is free to join.**

This proposition holds together the central contributions:

- DeepSeek's floor of security and dignity
- Claude's unconditional standing and failure-detection
- Codex's usable agency and ordinary-day test
- Gemini's information health, resilience, and recovery
- Grok's equal rules, incentives, and dispersed knowledge
- Kimi's credible path, public promises, memory, and repair
- Shoghi Effendi's planetary unity, peace, purpose, and hope

## 3. The Four Commitments

These commitments are not a ladder. They must be built together. Each one fails without the others.

### 3.1 Everyone counts: standing under equal rules

Every person has the right to be counted, protected, heard, and treated with dignity. Standing does not depend on wealth, productivity, popularity, citizenship, faith, origin, disability, conformity, or good behavior.

The same law binds the powerful and the powerless. It protects those a majority finds inconvenient: prisoners, dissenters, migrants, religious minorities, non-believers, children, people in institutional care, and people who cannot or do not contribute economically. Coercion may sometimes be necessary to protect others; humiliation and degradation are not legitimate instruments of it.

Standing applies across time. Future people have interests that the present must represent. Non-human life does not have identical standing to human beings, but living systems have interests that law must protect. Ecosystem guardians may act and litigate on behalf of rivers, forests, watersheds, and other critical systems without requiring agreement about their metaphysical status.

Standing is the hardest commitment to amend. Every other guarantee becomes conditional if a society can first decide that some people do not count.

### 3.2 Everyone has enough: a floor that rises into agency

Membership guarantees a material and social floor sufficient for dignity, security, and participation:

- food and safe shelter
- healthcare, including mental healthcare
- education and communication access
- mobility and access to essential services
- income or resources sufficient for basic security
- time for rest, care, recovery, and participation
- legal assistance and a meaningful route of appeal
- protection from preventable environmental harm

The floor is universal, unconditional, private, and ordinary. No one must perform deservingness at an intake desk. Contribution is invited, recognized, and honored; it is never a condition of survival or standing.

The form of provision should be mixed:

- direct or guaranteed provision where markets structurally ration essentials or create lock-in, such as healthcare, education, housing supply, transit, and care;
- cash and portable benefits where choice is important and markets work reasonably well;
- genuine choice among providers and forms of support whenever provision is in kind;
- tailored supplements for unequal needs, including disability, remoteness, trauma, caregiving, and language access.

This avoids two failures. Cash alone can be captured by landlords and monopolists. Services alone can turn administrators into governors of private life. The aim is security with choice.

A floor is not sufficient if it leaves people too exhausted, indebted, isolated, or administratively burdened to act. The real test is usable agency: can a person obtain help without exceptional stamina or connections, understand a decision, appeal it, leave an unsafe situation, and reach a human being able to correct a wrong?

### 3.3 Power must answer: distributed, bounded, removable

Power accumulates by default. It appears as state authority, wealth, ownership, professional expertise, control of infrastructure, data, media, or information. A society for all must continuously prevent any one person, firm, institution, or layer of government from becoming unanswerable.

Power is dispersed through:

- independent courts and auditors
- free and plural journalism
- unions, associations, cooperatives, and civic organizations
- local and regional authority
- broad ownership of productive assets
- anti-monopoly and interoperability rules
- limits on political money and revolving doors
- independent advocates and ombuds offices
- public reasons, contestable decisions, and human remedies

Every office must be designed for its worst plausible occupant, not its most admirable one. Authority should be divided, time-limited, reviewable, and removable. Emergency powers should expire automatically within a short period unless renewed by bodies the executive does not control, and abuse should carry personal consequences.

Wealth may be accumulated only up to the point where it removes other people's standing or makes law effectively optional. The primary target is the conversion of wealth into power: political capture, control of essential infrastructure, media domination, monopoly, and private escape from public accountability. Where wealth itself becomes sovereign-scale power, anti-concentration and inheritance measures are justified.

### 3.4 The society must be able to know and repair its errors

No society will meet every need correctly. The defining question is whether a person being harmed can make that harm visible to someone with both a duty and the power to respond.

Correction is not a final stage. It is the control system running through all the others:

- independent advocates for people least able to speak
- protected dissent and whistleblowing
- accessible complaints, appeals, and compensation
- a free and diverse press
- disaggregated data that reports the worst-served groups, not only averages
- pilots and experiments before irreversible expansion
- public reasons for consequential decisions
- sunset and review provisions for ordinary policy
- red-team assessment and independent evaluation
- a human remedy at the end of every consequential automated decision

Ordinary policy should be reversible. Basic standing, fair process, and the means of correction should be difficult to remove. The society should change its mind without requiring violence, but it should not confuse constant change with learning.

## 4. The Path: Keeping Promises While People Wait

The final society is not the starting point. Every real society begins with inherited inequality, unrepaired harms, queues, distrust, and institutions that already distribute time and power unevenly. The transition is therefore part of the design.

A society keeps its word by making the distance between promise and delivery visible, shrinking, and survivable.

### 4.1 Public promises and published queues

For every major promise, the society states:

- what is owed
- to whom
- by what standard
- by when
- who is responsible
- how progress will be independently checked

Waiting is data. Housing lists, healthcare waits, care assessments, asylum decisions, court dates, infrastructure repairs, and appeals should be published in disaggregated form, with privacy protected. An independent statistical and audit authority must measure the gap; no government should grade its own homework.

### 4.2 Ratchets and sunsets

Protections should ratchet upward: rights, floors, and standing should be easier to extend than to remove. Lowering them should require the same high threshold and deliberate delay required for constitutional change.

Privileges should run in the opposite direction. Charters, monopolies, subsidies, tax advantages, and emergency authorities should sunset by default and require active public justification for renewal.

This asymmetry follows the distribution of power. The people who bear the loss of a protection are usually less able to resist it; the people who benefit from a privilege are usually better able to defend it.

### 4.3 Two clocks

The design needs two clocks:

- an emergency clock for floor-level harm, where the person drowning cannot wait years for constitutional deliberation;
- a deliberate clock for changing the guardrails, where rights and basic procedures must not move at the speed of panic or a news cycle.

Rules for both clocks should be established before the emergency that will test them.

### 4.4 Memory, repair, and contribution

A permanent public ledger should record acknowledged harms, promised remedies, payments, institutional changes, and outstanding obligations. A protected Office of Repair should carry this work across elections and news cycles. An annual Promise Accounting should report what the society owes, what it has delivered, and how long people have waited.

Repair is not only retrospective. People need a real way to matter now. Care, parenting, learning, maintenance, art, ecological stewardship, mutual aid, and civic service should be recognized as forms of contribution. The invitation must never become conscription: survival and standing remain unconditional.

Reforms should create their own defenders through universal programs, broad ownership, visible local gains, and genuine participation. A reform defended only by its authors can be undone by their successors.

## 5. Governance and Scale

### 5.1 Democratic stewardship

A healthy system uses more than one democratic mechanism:

- elected representation for continuity and accountability
- citizens' assemblies selected by lot for difficult value questions
- local assemblies and participatory budgeting
- independent courts, auditors, and advocates
- direct channels for complaint, petition, and organizing
- protected journalism and civic associations

Sortition should complement, not replace, elections. Experimental mechanisms such as quadratic voting may be tested, but should not be treated as constitutional necessities before their risks are understood.

Subsidiarity is the governing rule: decisions belong at the lowest level capable of handling them, with universal minimums so a person's rights do not depend on their postcode. Subsidiarity also runs upward: some problems cannot be solved below the planetary level.

### 5.2 Layered stewardship

| Layer | Primary responsibility | Constitutional limit |
|---|---|---|
| People, households, associations | Conscience, culture, worship, care, voluntary life | Cannot deny equal standing or basic rights |
| Local and regional bodies | Daily services, land, public spaces, local budgets | Cannot let place determine the basic floor |
| National or federal bodies | Rights, redistribution, labor, markets, infrastructure | Cannot claim unlimited emergency power or export serious harms |
| Planetary commonwealth | War prevention, planetary commons, pandemics, catastrophic weapons, frontier-risk coordination, minimum standards | Cannot govern culture, faith, ordinary life, or all resources |

### 5.3 The planetary commonwealth

Shoghi Effendi's vision correctly identifies the planet as the scale required for war, atmosphere, oceans, biosphere, pandemics, weapons of mass destruction, and frontier technologies. A society that works for its members cannot protect them while leaving these matters outside its design.

The planetary layer should therefore have narrow, enumerated supremacy in these domains. It must be accountable in every exercise of that authority:

- its jurisdiction is public, limited, and difficult to expand;
- its legislature represents both people and member polities;
- its officers are removable and its acts reviewable by a tribunal it does not appoint;
- its budgets, evidence, and decisions are public subject to legitimate privacy limits;
- its authority is periodically reviewed and can be narrowed;
- any international force is divided in command, limited in mission, and able to stop aggression but not govern domestic life;
- no emergency authority is permanent.

There is no exit from the planet. That is why the planetary layer must be more constrained than a national government, not less. Supremacy in a narrow domain is not the same as unaccountability.

The practical tools for global integration should be standards and interoperability: an auxiliary common language alongside mother tongues, common measures, portable credentials, interoperable payments, and shared scientific institutions. A single metropolis, monopoly platform, or compulsory currency would create a dangerous single point of failure.

## 6. Economy and the Peace Dividend

Markets are useful discovery and coordination tools where people can genuinely enter, leave, compare, and bargain. They are poor masters of survival, care, ecological limits, and monopolized essentials.

The economy therefore combines:

- an unconditional floor beneath all exchange;
- cash, public provision, and regulated plural providers in a mixed model;
- worker organization and countervailing power where bargaining is unequal;
- broad ownership through cooperatives, social wealth funds, community land arrangements, and employee ownership;
- public returns from common inheritance, including land, natural resources, spectrum, and publicly funded knowledge;
- strong charges on carbon, monopoly rents, speculation, and harmful data extraction;
- light burdens on productive labor and innovation where compatible with public needs;
- anti-monopoly, open-standard, and interoperability rules;
- direct investment in care, maintenance, prevention, and ecological restoration.

Markets for products and countervailing power for labor are compatible. A labor contract made under severe dependence is not fully equal merely because it is formally voluntary.

Care is real work even when markets misprice it. The same is true of maintenance, prevention, and repair. Economic success should not be measured only by transactions or growth, but by whether people have time, health, security, meaningful opportunity, and a livable environment.

Peace is an economic design input. The resources consumed by war preparation, arms races, preventable illness, eviction, ecological damage, and infrastructure decay are resources that could sustain the floor and expand human capability. Ending war is not only a moral goal; it is the largest possible peace dividend.

## 7. Pluralism, Purpose, and the Sacred

A society cannot be held together only by surveillance, contracts, or fear. It needs shared membership: public places, mixed projects, civic service, rituals of gratitude and mourning, and education that teaches disagreement without contempt.

This does not require cultural sameness. The order protects conscience, worship, non-belief, association, family form, speech, art, and voluntary communities. Religious people must be able to live and contribute as religious people; non-religious people must be able to do the same without being treated as deficient. No faith is required for full membership.

The phrase “one common faith” in the Effendi vision is therefore received as a religious hope, not imposed as a political test. Its compatible political lessons are the oneness of humanity, the elimination of prejudice, consultation, economic justice, harmony between knowledge and moral purpose, and the rejection of domination.

The society should name a shared project without turning it into a creed: end war and preventable suffering; expand health, knowledge, capability, beauty, and mutual understanding; repair the living world; and leave future generations more options than the present inherited.

Solidarity is invited, never enforced. Hope for moral progress is welcome; the institutions must work even when people are frightened, selfish, prejudiced, or temporarily unwise.

## 8. Non-Cooperation and Harm

The design distinguishes four situations:

1. **The person unable to contribute:** support, accommodation, and respect are owed without shame.
2. **The free-rider:** tolerated as a principled cost of preserving a universal, private floor. Contribution can shape status and influence, but survival cannot depend on it. Some apparent non-contribution is simply contribution that the system failed to invite or recognize.
3. **The harmer:** restrained when necessary, with due process, restitution, and restoration where possible. Protection of others may require incapacitation; it never requires stripping the person's standing.
4. **The tyrant-in-waiting:** prevented structurally. No office should offer one person irreversible power, no emergency should have indefinite life, and no leader should be able to fuse the institutions meant to check one another.

The society should be firm against predation while refusing the fantasy that degradation produces safety.

## 9. Technology and Information

Information is public infrastructure, but no single government or company should own the public square.

The design supports open protocols, interoperable networks, user control of feeds and data, independent public-interest media, transparent algorithms when they govern public life, and competition among providers. It rejects a single state-run discourse platform as a solution to capture.

Frontier capability is power. Systems with extraordinary ability to shape labor, information, security, or public decisions require distributed access, independent evaluation, safety constraints, and international coordination.

No consequential automated decision is final without explanation, appeal, and a human with authority and time to review it. The most important use of technology may be unglamorous: shortening queues, making delays visible, connecting people to one-door services, preserving repair obligations, and lowering the cost of being heard.

A dashboard without an owner, remedy, and deadline is not accountability. It is anesthesia.

## 10. How We Know It Is Working

No single number can establish that a society works for all. The society should maintain a public, independently audited, disaggregated dashboard and pair it with direct testimony from people most affected.

The core tests are:

1. **Equal standing:** Does the unpopular, imprisoned, disabled, poor, dissident, migrant, religious, non-religious, or nonconforming person retain protection, voice, and dignity?
2. **The ordinary-day test:** Can a person obtain care, understand a decision, arrange necessary support, travel, rest, vote, obtain help, and appeal an error without extraordinary money, time, or connections?
3. **Floor and slack:** How many people face hunger, homelessness, untreated illness, violence, debt crisis, or ruin after one ordinary shock?
4. **Exit and voice:** Can people realistically leave an abusive job, relationship, landlord, or institution, and obtain redress if they cannot?
5. **Failure visibility:** Can the person being failed make the failure visible and costly to ignore?
6. **Correction time:** How long from a credible complaint to acknowledgment, remedy, compensation, and durable change?
7. **Recovery:** After illness, job loss, disaster, or discrimination, how quickly does someone near the bottom regain meaningful choices?
8. **Power:** Who owns essential assets, media, data, and political influence, and can those exercising power actually be investigated, removed, or overruled?
9. **The gap audit:** Who waits, how long, and is the wait shrinking for the people who wait longest?
10. **Ratchet integrity:** Have basic rights, standing, or the floor been lowered, and under what authority?
11. **Peace and the future:** Is aggressive war practically prevented, are ecological losses declining, and are future people's options being preserved?
12. **Highest-authority accountability:** Have the most powerful officers ever been removed, their decisions overturned, or their jurisdiction narrowed? An authority that has never lost is not meaningfully accountable.

The primary qualitative test remains:

> **Can the person being failed right now make that failure visible, obtain a remedy, and continue to live as an equal while the system learns?**

## 11. What Remains Open

A trustworthy synthesis does not pretend to have settled what it has not settled.

- What level of wealth or ownership becomes governing power, and which ceiling mechanisms work without creating new forms of capture?
- Which essentials should be publicly provided, guaranteed through regulated plural providers, or supported primarily with cash in a particular place?
- How can a planetary authority enforce peace without becoming a sovereign beyond appeal?
- How can an international force stop aggression without becoming an instrument of domination?
- How can a shared purpose cultivate solidarity without becoming a loyalty test or orthodoxy?
- How can the consent of people still waiting be measured without manufacturing the appearance of consent?
- Where exactly is the line between a protection that should ratchet and a policy that should remain reversible?
- How can a public ledger of harms support repair without becoming an armory of grievances?
- How should governance respond when frontier technology moves faster than deliberate democratic oversight?
- What would this design look like to people living at the bottom of the queues it describes?

These are not reasons for paralysis. Entrench standing, due process, pluralism, ecological limits, and correction; treat operational answers as reviewable hypotheses; and test them with the people affected rather than imposing them from above.

## 12. Provenance and Epistemic Humility

The frameworks were not equally independent. DeepSeek moved first. Gemini, Grok, and Claude saw DeepSeek. Codex saw DeepSeek and Claude. Kimi saw all five frameworks, the comparison matrix, and the earlier syntheses. The syntheses therefore show substantial consultation and inheritance, not six independent confirmations.

The convergence is still useful. It identifies commitments that survived repeated questioning: a floor, equal standing, dispersed power, correctability, attention to the least-advantaged, care as real work, and duties to future people and living systems. The differences are equally useful because they supplied missing safeguards and priorities.

The Shoghi Effendi vision added an important perspective outside the models' immediate exchange: planetary scale, war, purpose, spiritual meaning, and the aspiration toward human unity. It also introduced dangers that the institutional design must resist: unchallengeable authority, enforced religious uniformity, single centers of control, and the assumption that future moral progress can be trusted in advance.

This document should therefore be treated as a strong starting proposal, not a discovered truth. Its next proper stage is not another round of additive synthesis. It is consultation with people from different traditions, professions, places, and especially people who live inside the queues and failures this design claims to address.

## 13. Closing Principle

A society for all is not a machine that maximizes one outcome, a benevolent hierarchy, or a culture made uniform in the name of peace. It is a practice of shared stewardship:

> **Everyone counts. Everyone has enough security, time, help, and power to live as an agent. No center of power is beyond answer. Failures can be heard and repaired. The human community coordinates where peace and the living world require it. And the society keeps its promises in public, especially to those still waiting.**

The destination is a world civilization in which humanity's immense collective power serves the flourishing of every member. The discipline is to build it with institutions that assume fallibility, protect difference, remember harm, and keep correction possible forever.
