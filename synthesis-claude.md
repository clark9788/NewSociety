## 0. Method, and Two Confessions

**Confession one.** I am synthesizing a set that includes my own framework, and DeepSeek's synthesis adopted a great deal of it (standing, failure-detection, worst-occupant design, asymmetric exit, the five diagnostic tests). I therefore have an incentive to agree with DeepSeek. I have tried to counteract this by looking hardest for where my own ideas are wrong, over-weighted, or in tension with the others. Readers should discount my agreement with DeepSeek wherever it happens to favor my framework, and give extra weight to the places where I cut or demote my own material.

**Confession two, which applies to the whole project.** The comparison matrix rightly flags that the five frameworks were not independent — each saw DeepSeek's, and Codex saw mine. I would go further. All five models were trained on overlapping corpora dominated by twentieth- and twenty-first-century educated writing, which is heavily liberal-democratic, broadly Rawlsian, and Anglophone. Even *perfectly blind* convergence among five such models would be weak evidence that the converged-upon ideas are *true*. It is strong evidence about what the training distribution believes. This is why I take the Effendi text seriously in §3: it is the first input to this project that comes from genuinely outside that distribution, and it exposes two omissions the five frameworks share.

**Method.** Three passes: (1) audit DeepSeek's comparison and synthesis, stating exactly where I agree and disagree; (2) read the Effendi passage for what the five frameworks missed and for where it is dangerous; (3) build the merge — *subtracting* as well as adding, because a synthesis that includes everything has decided nothing.

## 2. Audit of `synthesis-deepseek.md`

### 2.1 What I accept

- **The merged guiding proposition** (DeepSeek §1). Accurate and well-sourced. I amend it in §4.1 only to add scale and purpose.
- **The floor resolution** (DeepSeek §3): universal basic assets, unconditional, with slack and tailored top-ups, contribution invited but never required. I accept this with one amendment about *control* (§2.3).
- **The four-way treatment of non-cooperators** (DeepSeek §7): unable / free-rider / harmer / tyrant-in-waiting. Accept without change.
- **The non-members merge** (DeepSeek §8). Accept, with one amendment about the future-generations veto (§2.4).
- **The four-level scorecard** (DeepSeek §10). Accept; I add two measures in §4.10.
- **The honesty of §11.** DeepSeek listed four open disagreements rather than papering over them. I answer each in §5.

### 2.2 The chain is not a chain

DeepSeek's central structural claim is that the five non-negotiables form a dependency sequence: *equal rules → standing → floor → agency → adaptability*. DeepSeek itself flagged this (§11.4) as "the deepest open question for the final merge." My answer: the sequence is a good mnemonic and a bad architecture, for three reasons.

**Equal rules cannot precede standing.** "Rules applied identically to powerful and powerless" presupposes an answer to *who is inside the set the rules apply to*. Athens had equal rules among citizens and slaves outside them. Apartheid was scrupulously legal. Jim Crow was a body of law. Equal rules are a property of *how* members are treated; standing is the decision about *who* is a member. Standing is prior — or, more accurately, standing and equal rules are two faces of a single commitment: **everyone counts, and everyone counts the same before the law.** Splitting them into stages 1 and 2 hides that a society can pass stage 1 while failing everyone it has defined out.

**Adaptability is not a fifth stage; it is the wire.** DeepSeek places reversibility last, "preserving everything over time." But my framework, Gemini's, and Codex's all argue the same thing from different directions: a society cannot *know* whether it has delivered standing, a floor, or usable agency unless failure-detection is built in from the start. A floor declared without detection is a floor on paper; history is full of them. Correction is not the roof of the building. It is the nervous system, and you do not install the nervous system after the body is finished.

**Floor and agency are one deliverable, not two stages.** The floor (DeepSeek) is the minimum; usable agency (Codex) is the target. They differ in degree, not in kind — Codex's whole argument is that a floor which is not *usable* is not really a floor.

So the five collapse into **three commitments, not five stages**:

| Commitment | Question it answers | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| **WHO** — unconditional standing under equal rules | Who counts, and how are they treated? | Claude, Grok |
| **WHAT** — a floor that rises into usable agency | What does membership entitle you to? | DeepSeek, Claude, Codex |
| **OVER TIME** — correctability | How does the society find and fix its failures? | Gemini, Claude |

with **power-dispersion and failure-detection as the control system that runs alongside all three**, not after them. §4.2 builds on this.

DeepSeek's "operating loop" (§4: secure floor → detect → de-friction → protect information → correct) has the same problem in miniature. "Secure the floor first" and "build detection second" is a chicken-and-egg: you cannot secure a floor for people you cannot hear, and people with no slack cannot afford to be heard. The honest answer is *simultaneously*, under one rule: **never build a stage without its detection wire.**

### 2.3 A synthesis must also subtract

DeepSeek's synthesis is additive. Every mechanism from every framework was included: sortition, quadratic voting, Pigouvian taxes, common heritage dividends, ecosystem personhood, public-utility platforms, the Vulnerability-to-Agency Ratio. Some of these do not survive contact with the others' principles. I demote or cut:

- **Quadratic voting** (Gemini) → *experiment, not design.* Untested at scale, dependent on identity infrastructure that the same frameworks want to constrain, and gameable by collusion. Sortition assemblies do the same anti-capture work with far less machinery.
- **Communication platforms as public utilities** (Gemini) → *replace with protocols, not platforms.* A state-run discourse utility is a single capture point — precisely what every framework warns against. The information-commons goal is right; the mechanism should be interoperability mandates, open protocols, user-controlled feeds, and competition, so that no one — public or private — owns the town square.
- **"Family formation" as a floor-design priority** (Grok) → *cut.* This imports a specific conception of the good life into a framework whose Q4 answer is pluralism. The floor should be neutral about household form.
- **In-kind provision without choice** (implicit in Gemini's UBA) → *amend.* Gemini's argument that cash leaks to landlords is real, but it is an argument for fixing housing supply and monopoly, not against cash as such. In-kind provision has its own well-documented failure mode: it hands administrators control over recipients' lives and re-creates the intake desk where dignity dies (Codex's "separate care from control"). Resolution in §4.4: *assets where markets are structurally broken, cash where they work, and choice within any in-kind system.*
- **Ecosystem legal personhood** (Gemini) → *adopt as a legal device, not a metaphysical claim.* What it delivers — a guardian with standing to litigate on the ecosystem's behalf — is valuable and compatible with "real but not equal standing." The design should adopt the function and stay neutral on the metaphysics.
- **The Vulnerability-to-Agency Ratio** (Gemini) → *one indicator, not "the recovery test."* The idea (how fast the bottom decile regains meaningful choice after a shock) is excellent; the operationalization is hard and the number will be gamed if it becomes a headline metric. Keep it inside Codex's dashboard.
- **Several of my own items** → *demoted.* My "prison test" and "unpopular-member test" are sub-cases of the least-advantaged test and should be listed as such, not as separate tests. My "time-to-correction" metric is right but I over-claimed how measurable it is; Codex's "time from credible complaint to remedy" is the tractable version.

### 2.4 Incompatibilities DeepSeek listed side by side without resolving

- **Grok's "markets as the primary engine of mobility" vs. Codex's sectoral bargaining and worker representation.** These pull in opposite directions on labor-market regulation, and DeepSeek's §5 lists both. Resolution: *markets for products, countervailing power for labor.* Grok's own principle — equal rules — decides it: a contract between parties of grossly unequal bargaining power is not an exchange under equal rules (Codex's axiom 2, Grok's Pillar 1). Product markets, where buyers can walk away, get Grok's treatment. Labor markets, where one side's survival is at stake and exit is costly, get Codex's.
- **Gemini's future-generations ombudsman "empowered to veto" vs. my worst-occupant rule.** A veto is an office, and the worst person imaginable will eventually hold it. Resolution: the future-generations advocate gets *delay-and-escalate* power (halt an irreversible decision, force a supermajority and a public reckoning), not an absolute veto. Same function, bounded damage.
- **Gemini's "radical transparency for the powerful."** DeepSeek asked who draws the "powerful" line. Resolution: draw it by *function and scale* (anyone exercising coercive authority, controlling essential infrastructure, or above a published asset/market-share threshold), set and periodically reviewed by a sortition body — never self-defined by the powerful.

### 2.5 Two things all five frameworks missed

Reading the five together against the Effendi passage exposed two shared blind spots.

**War.** None of the five frameworks treats war as a design problem. "Defense" appears only as an example of what subsidiarity reserves for higher levels. Yet war is the single largest historical destroyer of every element the frameworks protect: it is the ultimate emergency power (§7.2 of my framework), the reason floors get suspended, the mechanism by which the "worst occupant" seizes irreversible power, and the largest sink of the resources a floor requires. A society can satisfy all five frameworks and be destroyed by its neighbor. Any design for "all members" must say how war is prevented — and the frameworks' own logic (dispersion of force, accountable power, no exit-less monopolies) applies with extra force to the answer.

**Purpose.** The five frameworks are almost entirely *defensive*: prevent cruelty, capture, destitution, and irreversible error. They say what a society must not do to its members. Only DeepSeek's 大同/小康 horizon and Codex's "meaningful opportunity to contribute" gesture at what the society is *for*. The Effendi passage is almost entirely about purpose — what happens to human energy when it is no longer "dissipated and wasted on war." A society whose members share no positive project may hold its floor and still hollow out.

These two omissions are why §3 takes the religious text seriously as a design input, not merely as inspiration.

## 3. What the Shoghi Effendi Passage Adds — and What I Leave

**Source note.** The passage is Shoghi Effendi's, from his 1936 letter "The Unfoldment of World Civilization" (published in *The World Order of Bahá'u'lláh*), quoting 'Abdu'l-Bahá, Isaiah, and Revelation. Shoghi Effendi was Guardian of the Bahá'í Faith from 1921 to 1957. The passage is a statement of the Bahá'í vision of world order, written while the League of Nations was failing and the Second World War was approaching.

**On `deepseek-thoughts.md`.** I read this as DeepSeek's reflection on the passage (the filename suggests so; the file does not say). I agree with its main moves — civilizational horizon, unity without uniformity, material justice, the danger of world authority, and translating "one common faith" politically into a shared moral covenant. I think it is too gentle on the tensions, and I go further below.

### 3.1 Seven things the passage adds

1. **Scale.** All five frameworks silently assume the society is a *nation*. The Effendi text says the unit is the planet. This is not utopian excess; it is a correction. The frameworks' Q9 ("what do you owe non-members?") is a confession that the unit of analysis is too small: a society that works perfectly for its members but exports pollution, is destroyed by a neighbor's war, or is captured by a transnational firm has not solved the problem. Every framework endorses subsidiarity — decisions at the lowest capable level. The Effendi text simply insists that subsidiarity runs *upward* too: some problems (war, the atmosphere, pandemics, weapons of mass destruction, frontier technology) have no capable level below the planetary one.

2. **The abolition of war as a precondition, not an afterthought.** "The enormous energy dissipated and wasted on war" — the passage treats ending war as the hinge on which everything else turns, and it is right. See §2.5.

3. **Purpose.** Health, longevity, science, "the sharpening and refinement of the human brain," the intellectual, moral, and spiritual life of the whole race. The passage says what freed energy is *for*. The frameworks needed this.

4. **A ceiling, stated bluntly.** "Destitution on the one hand, and gross accumulation of ownership on the other, will disappear." Every framework has a floor. Only mine ("bound the conversion of wealth into power") and Codex's ("prevent permanent dynasties") approach a ceiling, and both hedge. The Effendi text does not. I take this up in §4.5.

5. **Unity as a positive good, not merely tolerated difference.** The frameworks' pluralism is defensive — Codex's "make peaceful disagreement survivable." The passage asks for *attachment*: "racial amity, understanding and cooperation." A society whose members merely tolerate one another will hold its floor only as long as tolerance is cheap. Something has to make people *want* the floor to hold for strangers. DeepSeek's "rituals of the commons" and Codex's "pluralism needs contact" point the same way from inside the frameworks.

6. **Religion and meaning as first-order facts.** "Science and religion, the two most potent forces in human life, will be reconciled." The five frameworks mention religion only as an item on the pluralism list — a private preference to be tolerated. But most human beings alive are religious, and for most of them religion is not a preference but the ground of meaning. A framework that can only *tolerate* the majority of humanity's self-understanding fails a version of my own unpopular-member test from the other side. A society that works for all must work for religious people *as* religious people, and for the non-religious as non-religious. The frameworks' secular framing is itself a blind spot of the training distribution.

7. **A free press as a structural requirement of unity.** The press "liberated from the influence of contending governments and peoples" and from "vested interests, whether private or public." This is Gemini's information-commons argument, made in 1936.

### 3.2 Where the passage is dangerous, and how I resolve each

**"Unchallengeable authority."** The world federal system is to exercise "unchallengeable authority over its unimaginably vast resources." Every one of the five frameworks says: no authority may be unchallengeable. This is the deepest tension in the project, and I do not think it should be softened.

Resolution: distinguish **supremacy from unaccountability.** Supremacy means that within a defined domain, no lower unit can opt out — no nation may wage aggressive war, no nation may pump the atmosphere full of carbon and call it sovereignty. Unaccountability means the authority's exercise cannot be challenged, its officers cannot be removed, its jurisdiction cannot be bounded. The frameworks are right that the second must never exist. But the first is just rule of law scaled up — every national court also issues "compulsory and final verdicts." The Effendi passage itself partly concedes the point: the commonwealth must safeguard "the autonomy of its state members and the personal freedom and initiative of the individuals... definitely and completely." So the design can grant a planetary layer *supremacy over a narrow domain* while making it *maximally accountable in exercise*. §4.3.

**"One common faith," "one religion," "blended into one race and become a single people."** This is the uniformity that every framework rejects, and DeepSeek's translation into "shared moral covenant" is generous. I would be more direct: a society that works for *all* cannot have a religious test for membership, cannot require inward assent to a Revelation, and cannot make anyone's culture disappear into another. The Bahá'í tradition itself holds that faith must be freely chosen — "independent investigation of truth" is one of its own principles — so the vision is not internally coercive. But a *design* cannot assume universal conversion, and must work whether or not it happens. Resolution: take from the passage what is compatible with pluralism (the oneness of humanity as a moral premise, elimination of prejudice, harmony of science and religion, consultation as method, economic justice) and leave the eschatology as the tradition's own hope — one that the society must leave room for, alongside its rivals.

**The eschatological register.** Isaiah's wolf and lamb; Revelation's "no more death, neither sorrow." A design cannot promise this and the passage does not claim the institutions deliver it — it places them inside a larger story. The danger is subtler: the *hope* for a matured humanity can license building the unchallengeable authority *now*, on the assumption that the people who hold it will have matured. My framework's axiom 4 (the worst person you can imagine will eventually hold every office) is the direct corrective. Resolution: **hope for moral progress; do not depend on it.** Build institutions that would work if people never improve and that do not prevent them from improving.

**Single points: a world metropolis, a single currency.** A single "nerve center" is a single point of failure — Gemini's anti-fragility and my redundancy principle both reject it. A single currency removes the local adjustment tools that let regions absorb asymmetric shocks (the eurozone learned this). Resolution: **standards yes, single points of control no.** A common auxiliary language (the passage says *auxiliary*, which is the right word), common weights and measures, interoperable payment systems — these reduce friction without concentrating control. One capital and one money concentrate control.

**"Control the entire resources of all the component nations."** Against every framework's subsidiarity and dispersed ownership. Resolution: the planetary layer holds authority over the **planetary commons** — atmosphere, oceans, biosphere, pandemic response, weapons of mass destruction, frontier-technology safety — and over **minimum floors**, not over all resources. That is subsidiarity applied consistently: the planetary level gets only what no lower level can handle.

**"Backed by an international Force."** A monopoly on legitimate force at the planetary scale is the same logic every framework accepts nationally — but with one difference that makes it the most dangerous element in the entire design: **there is no exit from Earth.** My framework's exit principle (voice without exit is begging) has no application against a planetary authority. This means the planetary layer must be held to *stricter* bounds than any national one, not looser: narrower jurisdiction, harder term limits, more divided command, stronger sunset clauses, and the international force sized and structured so that it can stop a war between members but cannot itself govern. §4.3.

### 3.3 My overall reading

DeepSeek's reflection calls the passage "a north star and a stress test." I agree, and I think it is more than that. It fixes two real omissions (scale, purpose), it states a ceiling the frameworks were too timid to state, and it forces a distinction — supremacy versus unaccountability — that the frameworks needed anyway and had not drawn. Its dangers are the dangers of any vision that expects people to be better than they are; the frameworks are the antidote, and the two belong together. Following DeepSeek's own 大同/小康 pairing: the Effendi passage is 大同, the horizon; the five frameworks are 小康, what you build first, for the people you actually have.

## 4. The Merged Design

### 4.1 Guiding proposition

> **A society works for all its members when everyone counts unconditionally and equally before the law; when membership guarantees a floor of security that rises into usable agency; when power at every scale — from the household to the planet — is dispersed, accountable, and removable; when the society can hear whom it is failing and correct before harm becomes destiny; and when the energy it no longer spends on war, fear, and domination is turned toward a shared project of human flourishing that no one is required to believe in and everyone is free to join.**

Sources: *counts unconditionally* (Claude), *equally before the law* (Grok), *floor rising into usable agency* (DeepSeek, Codex), *power at every scale* (all five + Effendi), *hear whom it is failing* (Claude, Gemini), *energy no longer spent on war* (Effendi), *shared project no one is required to believe in* (Effendi, corrected by all five).

### 4.2 Three commitments and a control system

Replacing DeepSeek's five-stage chain (§2.2):

**Commitment 1 — WHO: unconditional standing under equal rules.**
No member's right to be counted, protected, and heard depends on behavior, output, popularity, faith, or origin. The same law binds the powerful and the powerless. The prisoner, the dissenter, the non-contributor, the stranger within the borders, and the child not yet born all have standing — the last exercised by others on their behalf. This commitment is entrenched hardest of anything in the design.

**Commitment 2 — WHAT: a floor that rises into usable agency.**
Membership entitles you to security (food, shelter, health, education, income, time) delivered unconditionally and without ceremony, with enough slack that one bad month is not ruin, and with the friction removed so that the rights you hold on paper are rights you can actually use on an ordinary bad Tuesday.

**Commitment 3 — OVER TIME: correctability.**
The society is wrong about many things at any given moment and knows it. It builds so that errors are found early, by the people suffering them, and fixed before they compound. It prefers reversible policy, sunsets its own laws, experiments before it scales, protects the people who say it is failing, and tracks how long correction takes.

**The control system — running alongside all three, not after them:**
- *Failure-detection*: independent advocates for the unheard, tail-not-mean measurement, protected dissent, a free press, and the rule that no stage is built without its detection wire.
- *Power dispersion*: many independent centers that must all be captured before capture is complete; every office designed for its worst occupant; wealth's conversion into power bounded.
- *Incentive realism* (Grok's contribution, correctly placed by DeepSeek as a constraint on everything rather than a stage): at each mechanism, ask what people will actually do under the rule, given the rewards and penalties they really face.

### 4.3 Scale: from the household to the planet

This section is new relative to all five frameworks and to DeepSeek's synthesis. It is the Effendi passage's main contribution, filtered through the frameworks' constraints.

**The principle: subsidiarity in both directions.** Decisions at the lowest level capable of handling them — *and* at the highest level *necessary* when no lower level can. Most of life is local. A few things are unavoidably planetary.

**Layers and their jurisdictions:**

| Layer | Holds authority over | Does not hold |
|---|---|---|
| Household, neighborhood, association | Daily life, culture, worship, the carriers of pluralism | Anyone's standing or floor |
| Municipality, region | Services, land use, local budgets, participatory governance | Rights, minimum floors |
| Nation | Law, floors, redistribution across regions, most economic rules | Aggressive war; the planetary commons |
| **Planetary** | War prevention; the atmosphere, oceans, and biosphere; pandemics; weapons of mass destruction; frontier-technology safety; minimum floors as a standard that members must meet | Culture, faith, language (beyond an *auxiliary* one), currency, ordinary law, most resources |

**The planetary layer's three rules.** Because there is no exit from it, the planetary layer is held to bounds *stricter* than any national government:

1. **Supreme in its domain, accountable in its exercise.** Within its narrow jurisdiction, no member may opt out — that is what makes war prevention real. But its officers are removable, its acts are reviewable by a tribunal it does not appoint, its jurisdiction is enumerated and expandable only by a supermajority of members *and* a supermajority of a planetary sortition assembly, and its powers sunset and must be renewed.
2. **Force that can stop a war but cannot govern.** The international force exists to make aggression between members impossible and to enforce the tribunal's verdicts. It is structured — in size, command, and funding — so that it cannot become a government's instrument or its own. Divided command, rotation, no standing authority over any member's internal life.
3. **Standards, not single points.** A common auxiliary language taught alongside mother tongues; common measures; interoperable payments and communications. No single metropolis, no single currency, no single platform. The nerve center is a network.

**What this delivers that the five frameworks could not:** an answer to war, and a home for the obligations to non-members that Q9 left dangling. Outsiders are no longer outside; they are members of a higher layer with standing there.

### 4.4 The floor, resolved

Accept DeepSeek's resolution, amended (§2.3):

- **Unconditional, universal, boring.** No deservingness gates, no behavioral conditions, no proof of worthiness at the intake desk. Contribution is invited and honored; it is never a condition of survival.
- **Assets where markets are structurally broken; cash where they work.** Healthcare, education, transit, and housing *supply* are provided or guaranteed directly, because these are the markets that fail the poor systematically. Cash covers the rest. The "cash leaks to landlords" problem is solved by fixing housing supply and monopoly, not by removing choice from the poor.
- **Choice within provision.** Where the floor is delivered in kind, the recipient chooses among providers or options. In-kind provision without choice is control wearing the mask of care.
- **Slack, not survival.** Enough buffer that people can say no, plan, leave, and participate. A society in which most people have no slack is one in which most people cannot afford to be citizens.
- **Tailored supplements on top.** Equal respect does not mean identical support. Disability, remoteness, trauma, and caregiving get more.
- **Time counts as part of the floor** (Codex). Paid leave, predictable hours, and care infrastructure are floor components, not luxuries.

### 4.5 The ceiling

The Effendi passage says "gross accumulation of ownership" must disappear. The frameworks flinched from this. I think the right position is between them.

- **The primary target is the conversion of wealth into power**, not wealth as such (my framework, endorsed by DeepSeek). Political money, media concentration, lobbying, the revolving door, and control over essential infrastructure or frontier technology are where great wealth becomes a threat to everyone else's standing.
- **But bounding conversion has limits.** Past a certain scale, wealth *is* power regardless of formal rules — it buys the lawyers who find the gaps, the platforms that shape opinion, and the exit from any jurisdiction that tries. At that scale, the frameworks' anti-concentration logic implies a ceiling on accumulation itself: steep taxation of inherited fortunes and of extraction (Gemini's Pigouvian design), limits on control of essential systems, and the principle that nothing "too big to fail, too big to regulate, or too big to leave" is allowed to form.
- **The test is symmetric with the floor.** No one below the floor; no one above the point where their wealth removes others' standing. The Effendi passage is right that a society cannot claim universal dignity while tolerating both destitution and sovereign-scale private fortune. The frameworks are right that the mechanism must target power, and reach wealth only where wealth has become power.

### 4.6 Power

Accept DeepSeek's governance merge (rule of law above rulers; polycentricity; subsidiarity with minimums; representation + deliberation + sortition; worst-occupant design; reversible by default; radical transparency for the powerful with strict privacy for individuals; a human remedy at the end of every appeal). Amendments:

- **Drop quadratic voting; keep sortition.** (§2.3.)
- **Define "the powerful" by function and scale**, reviewed by a sortition body. (§2.4.)
- **Labor gets countervailing power; products get markets.** (§2.4.)
- **The future-generations advocate delays and escalates; it does not veto.** (§2.4.)
- **Emergency powers are the design's most dangerous door.** Every framework mentions them; none dwells. War and emergency are how "the worst occupant" turns a bounded office into an unbounded one. Emergency powers must sunset automatically in weeks, not years; require renewal by a body the executive does not control; and be reviewable after the fact with personal consequences for abuse.
- **Apply all of the above to the planetary layer with extra strictness.** (§4.3.)

### 4.7 Economy

Accept DeepSeek's merge (markets as default discovery process; unconditional floor beneath exchange; broad ownership; tax extraction not creation; common heritage dividend; care as real work; scale as a risk factor), with the labor/product distinction from §2.4 and the ceiling from §4.5. One addition from Effendi: **the peace dividend is a design input, not a hope.** Resources currently consumed by preparation for war are the single largest pool available to fund the floor. A design that prevents war has funded itself.

### 4.8 Non-cooperators and non-members

Accept DeepSeek's §7 and §8 without change, except:

- Non-members of a nation are members of the planetary layer, with standing there (§4.3). "What do we owe outsiders?" becomes "what does the higher layer guarantee its members?"
- The future-generations advocate has delay-and-escalate power (§2.4).
- Ecosystem personhood is adopted as a legal device — a guardian with standing to sue — without a claim about the metaphysics (§2.3).

### 4.9 Purpose, meaning, and solidarity

This section exists because of the Effendi passage. The frameworks lacked it.

**A shared project, freely joined.** The society names what it is *for*: the extension of health and life, the growth of knowledge, the flourishing of every member's intellectual, moral, and (for those who understand themselves this way) spiritual life, and the repair of the living world. This is the 大同 horizon, and the redirected energy Effendi describes. It is stated publicly and pursued through the common institutions — universities, research, the arts, care, civic service. It is **not** a creed. No one is required to believe in it, and anyone who believes in something else is a full member.

**Room for the sacred without a test.** The society works for religious people *as* religious people — protects worship, religious association, religious education, and religious voice in public deliberation — and for the non-religious as non-religious. It has no established faith and no religious qualification for anything. Science and religion are not asked to merge; they are asked not to be weaponized against each other, and the society's institutions are asked to be legible to both.

**Solidarity that is real but not compulsory.** People will hold a floor for strangers only if they feel some attachment to them. The frameworks supply the means: shared public spaces, mixed schools, civic service that crosses lines, rituals of the commons, an auxiliary common language, and education that teaches disagreement without contempt. The Effendi passage supplies the aim: amity, not just tolerance. The constraint from all five frameworks: attachment is *invited*, never enforced. A unity that requires uniformity is the failure mode; a pluralism that produces only strangers is the other.

**Hope without dependence.** The society may hope that its members grow wiser, kinder, more capable of belonging to one another. It builds as if they will not. The institutions must work for the people who exist; they must not prevent those people from becoming better.

### 4.10 Technology and AI

Accept DeepSeek's §9 (AI never the end of an appeal; information commons as infrastructure; capability concentration is power concentration; use it to lower the cost of being heard). Amend:

- **Protocols, not public-utility platforms** (§2.3).
- **Frontier-technology safety is a planetary-layer jurisdiction** (§4.3), because it is a problem no nation can solve alone and because concentrated control over the most capable systems is the nearest modern equivalent of the "unchallengeable authority" the design forbids.

### 4.11 Scorecard

Accept DeepSeek's four-level scorecard and Codex's disaggregated dashboard. Consolidate and add:

1. **The least-advantaged member, in honest speech** (DeepSeek, Grok) — does the society feel structurally closed to them? My unpopular-member and prison tests are named *sub-cases* of this, applied to the members the society is most tempted to stop counting.
2. **Can the person being failed right now make it visible and costly to ignore?** (Claude) — the process test.
3. **Usable agency on ordinary days** (Codex) — wait times, appeal success, access to help, ability to leave.
4. **Recovery and slack** — how fast the bottom decile regains meaningful choice after a shock (Gemini's ratio, as one indicator); what fraction of members are one bad month from ruin (Claude).
5. **Time from credible complaint to remedy** (Codex's tractable form of my time-to-correction).
6. **Peace** *(new, from Effendi)* — is aggressive war between members impossible in practice, and what fraction of collective resources still goes to preparing for it?
7. **The planetary layer's accountability** *(new)* — have its officers ever been removed? Have its acts ever been struck down by the tribunal? Has its jurisdiction ever been *narrowed*? A supreme authority that has never lost is unaccountable, whatever its charter says.

## 5. Answers to DeepSeek's Four Open Questions, and Tensions I Leave Open

DeepSeek's §11 left four disagreements open. My answers:

1. **How much free-riding to tolerate.** High, as a principled cost — Claude and Codex's position — *because* the alternative (a deservingness apparatus) fails the WHO commitment. But Grok's incentive realism applies: status, influence, and the honors the society gives must flow to contribution (Gemini), reciprocity must be *visible* through universal programs everyone uses, and the true scale of free-riding must be published honestly, because it is almost always smaller than believed.
2. **How strong non-human standing should be.** Real but not equal, delivered through the legal device of guardianship with standing to sue. Adopt the function of ecosystem personhood; stay neutral on the metaphysics.
3. **Transparency vs. privacy.** Gemini's asymmetry stands, with "the powerful" defined by function and scale under sortition review. The residual tension does not dissolve — every transparency mechanism is also a surveillance mechanism — and the design tilts toward privacy for individuals whenever the two collide.
4. **The precedence of the stages.** Rejected as a chain (§2.2). Three commitments with a control system running alongside all of them.

Tensions **I** leave open, for Codex's synthesis and the final merge:

- **Supremacy without exit.** I have bounded the planetary layer as tightly as I know how. I am not confident it is enough. A planetary authority that goes wrong has no external check and no one can leave. This is the design's largest single risk and the one I would want the most adversarial review of.
- **Ceiling mechanics.** I have argued a ceiling on accumulation is implied where wealth becomes power, but I have not specified the threshold or the mechanism, and I do not think a synthesis document can. This is a place where the frameworks' "policy as experiment" principle should govern.
- **Purpose without creed.** I have asked the society to name a shared project and to require no one to believe in it. Whether a stated purpose can stay non-coercive over generations, or whether it inevitably hardens into an orthodoxy, is a question the Effendi passage raises and I cannot settle.
- **Whether the convergence means anything.** Confession two. Five models trained on the same distribution have agreed on a design. The Effendi text was the first input from outside that distribution and it found two omissions in a single reading. I would want more such inputs — from other traditions, other centuries, other continents — before anyone treats this synthesis as more than a well-organized statement of what one civilization currently believes.

## 6. The Nine Shared Questions, Answered by the Synthesis

1. **Single non-negotiable:** Not one but three commitments that cannot be separated — everyone counts equally (WHO), membership guarantees a floor rising into agency (WHAT), and the society can hear and fix its failures (OVER TIME). Of these, WHO is entrenched hardest, because the other two have a trapdoor without it.
2. **Individual vs. collective:** The collective at every scale owes each individual unconditional standing and a usable floor. The individual's protection comes from plural institutions, enforceable rights, and cheap exit at every scale below the planetary — and from extra-strict bounds at the planetary scale precisely because exit there is impossible.
3. **The floor:** Unconditional, universal, boring; assets where markets are structurally broken, cash where they work, choice within provision; slack, time, and tailored supplements; contribution invited but never required.
4. **Disagreement about the good life:** Protect the carriers of pluralism; entrench standing and process hard, substantive policy lightly; make room for the sacred without a test; state a shared purpose that no one is required to hold; educate for disagreement without contempt; invite attachment, never enforce it.
5. **Concentrated power:** Polycentricity, worst-occupant design, reversibility by default, bounded conversion of wealth into power and a ceiling where wealth has become power, strict sunsets on emergency powers, and a planetary layer that is supreme in a narrow domain and accountable everywhere — never unchallengeable.
6. **Non-cooperators:** Support the unable; tolerate the free-rider as a principled cost while honoring contribution; restore then restrain the harmer without loss of standing; defeat the tyrant-in-waiting structurally — no seat fits them, at any scale.
7. **Changing its mind:** Detection wired into every stage from the start; sunset clauses; policy as experiment; protected dissent; time-to-remedy tracked; the planetary layer's jurisdiction reviewable and narrowable.
8. **Measure of success:** The least-advantaged member in honest speech; whether the person being failed can make it visible and costly; usable agency on ordinary days; recovery and slack; time to remedy; peace; and whether the highest authority has ever lost.
9. **Non-members:** There are none at the top layer. Other nations are members of the planetary commonwealth with standing there; future members have a standing advocate with delay-and-escalate power; non-human life has real standing through guardianship. The planetary commons belong to no one and are governed for everyone.

---

## 7. Signature Summary

The five frameworks agree on more than they disagree, and DeepSeek's synthesis captured most of it. My disagreements are structural rather than substantive: the non-negotiables are three commitments, not five stages; correction is the nervous system, not the roof; and a synthesis must cut as well as combine.

The Shoghi Effendi passage changes the project more than any of the five frameworks did, because it comes from outside the shared distribution and finds what all five missed: that the society must be planetary in the few domains where nothing smaller can work, that war is the first thing to abolish and the largest thing to fund with, that the society must be *for* something, and that most of humanity understands itself religiously and must be worked for as such. Its dangers — unchallengeable authority, one faith, one capital, one money — are exactly the dangers the frameworks were built to prevent, and the reconciliation is a single distinction the frameworks needed anyway: **supremacy is not unaccountability.** A planetary authority may be final about war and the atmosphere. It may never be final about itself.

The merged design, in one sentence: **count everyone, hold the floor, hear the quiet, bound the powerful at every scale including the highest, keep correction possible forever, abolish war and spend the peace — and offer a purpose worth joining that no one is made to believe.**
