## 2. The Nine Shared Questions, Side by Side

### Q1 — Single non-negotiable element

| Model | Answer |
|---|---|
| DeepSeek | The floor of security & dignity (among four non-negotiable pillars). |
| Claude | Unconditional standing — the right to be counted, protected, and heard regardless of behavior, output, or popularity. |
| Codex | Usable agency — a real, not theoretical, ability to shape one's life and challenge institutions. |
| Gemini | Systemic reversibility & adaptability — the ability to peacefully rewrite rules, change models, and unseat leaders. |
| Grok | Equal rules + agency under them (security under law that applies equally). |
| Kimi | The credible path in public — the temporal form of unconditional standing; if forced to choose among the five, adopts Claude's standing as logically prior. |

### Q2 — Individual vs. collective

| Model | Answer |
|---|---|
| DeepSeek | Relational personhood + enforceable rights: the self is a knot in a web of duties, but dignity/voice are rights-fortified against the whole. |
| Claude | The collective owes every individual unconditional standing and a floor; protection comes from plural independent institutions + cheap exit — rights are enforced, not granted. |
| Codex | A wide protected sphere of personal liberty; the collective supplies the material, social, and institutional conditions that make liberty real. |
| Gemini | Inviolable negative rights (thought, speech, body, movement) + relational duty; the collective cannot override core rights, and the individual cannot use liberty to destroy the commons. |
| Grok | Individual rights as hard constraints; cooperation flows from reciprocity — protect the individual's capacity to act and to speak. |
| Kimi | The consensus (standing + usable floor + plural institutions + cheap exit); the individual is *asked* for patience but never *owes* it — the collective owes the wait to be survivable, visible, and finite. |

### Q3 — The floor

| Model | Answer |
|---|---|
| DeepSeek | Unconditional floor: food, shelter, healthcare, and income sufficient for dignity. |
| Claude | Unconditional and boring; slack (buffer) not just survival; no deservingness gates, because gates fail exactly where the weakest stand. |
| Codex | Universal basic security with enough slack, plus tailored support for unequal needs (disability, remoteness, trauma). |
| Gemini | Universal Basic Assets over UBI alone: direct access to goods (housing, healthcare, education, transit) + a moderate cash baseline. |
| Grok | A genuine floor of basic physical security; prefer work/contribution/family formation over pure transfers where possible. |
| Kimi | The merged form — unconditional, universal, boring; assets where markets are structurally broken, cash where they work, choice within provision; slack, time, tailored supplements — held by a constitutional ratchet, with queues published. |

### Q4 — Disagreement about the good life

| Model | Answer |
|---|---|
| DeepSeek | Unity without uniformity (和而不同): one civic identity layered over many personal/cultural identities; protect difference. |
| Claude | Protect the *carriers* of pluralism (associations, communities), not just permission; entrench standing/process hard, policy lightly; educate for disagreement without contempt. |
| Codex | Maximum room for difference, firm limits on domination and preventable harm; pluralism needs contact as well as tolerance. |
| Gemini | Subsidiarity + modular communities: high-level human-rights standards with strong local autonomy, and mobility to choose between different ways of life. |
| Grok | Shared civic identity layered over private ones; no enforced cultural uniformity; individuals pursue different conceptions without harming others. |
| Kimi | The consensus (protect the carriers of pluralism; standing/process entrenched hard, policy lightly; the sacred without a test); adds: the path itself must not harden into an orthodoxy — progress is a direction, not a destination creed. |

### Q5 — Preventing concentrated power from capturing the design

| Model | Answer |
|---|---|
| DeepSeek | Dispersion, transparency, revisability; hard checks on state, corporate, and algorithmic power. |
| Claude | Polycentricity — many independent centers that must *all* be captured; bound the conversion of wealth into power; design every office for its worst occupant; reversible by default. |
| Codex | Disperse ownership and authority; regulate political money and monopolies; protect organization and journalism; independent review; human remedy. |
| Gemini | Radical transparency for the powerful paired with strict privacy for individuals; quadratic voting and sortition; anti-monopoly enforcement. |
| Grok | Dispersion by function, level, and time; transparency and contestability; exit options; constitutional hardness on rights. |
| Kimi | The consensus (polycentricity, worst-occupant design, reversibility, bounded wealth→power, ceilings, sunset emergency powers) + two temporal constraints: no power may cancel the promises; no power may grade its own gap. |

### Q6 — People who will not cooperate

| Model | Answer |
|---|---|
| DeepSeek | *(Posed as open question; no explicit section.)* Implicit: design for the worst, invite the best; hard checks. |
| Claude | Three types: free-rider tolerated (floor makes it cheap); harmer restored then incapacitated but never stripped of standing; tyrant-in-waiting defeated structurally — no seat fits them. |
| Codex | Three types: unable→support & accommodation; free-rider→tolerate more than punitive systems do; harmer→firm but humane restraint, restitution and restoration where possible. |
| Gemini | Free-riders: survival decoupled from status, so prestige/influence require contribution; cheaters & tyrants: accountability, anti-monopoly, and restorative isolation before they capture leverage points. |
| Grok | *(Implicit; no explicit section.)* Incentives + reciprocity/reputation punish free-riding; predators prosecuted under equal rules. |
| Kimi | The consensus four-way distinction (support the unable; tolerate the free-rider; restore then restrain the harmer without loss of standing; defeat the tyrant structurally); adds: much free-riding is uninvited contribution — build the invitation channels first, count honestly, tolerate the rest. |

### Q7 — How the society changes its own mind

| Model | Answer |
|---|---|
| DeepSeek | Revisability: constitutional basics hard to change, ordinary policy easy; adapt peacefully rather than by violence. |
| Claude | Sunset clauses, policy experiments, cheap reversibility, protected dissent, and time-to-correction as a tracked metric. |
| Codex | Memory + revision: rights-protected experimentation, public evidence, citizen deliberation, independent evaluation, and repair/redress. |
| Gemini | Sunset clauses on major legislation + periodic citizens' assemblies re-evaluating constitutional assumptions against empirical data. |
| Grok | Evidence-based rebalancing; scientific method; hard rights, soft policy; peaceful revision of ordinary policy. |
| Kimi | The consensus (detection wired in, sunsets, experiments, protected dissent, time-to-remedy) + memory institutions so change does not mean amnesia; protections ratchet upward, ordinary policies reverse. |

### Q8 — Measure of success for "working for all"

| Model | Answer |
|---|---|
| DeepSeek | The least-advantaged member test, plus no one below the floor, real mobility, high trust, actual voice, shared wellbeing, peaceful adaptation. |
| Claude | "Can the person being failed right now make that failure visible and costly to ignore?" + unpopular-member, prison, exit, slack, and time-to-correction tests. |
| Codex | Usable agency on ordinary days, judged by the worst-served group (the moral audit), via a public, disaggregated dashboard. |
| Gemini | The Vulnerability-to-Agency Ratio: how fast someone at the bottom 10th percentile recovers meaningful choice after a crisis. |
| Grok | The least-advantaged member — asked in conditions of honest speech — plus mobility, trust, open exit/voice, and peaceful revision. |
| Kimi | The merged scorecard, plus: the gap audit (who waits, how long, is it shrinking), ratchet integrity, the consent of the waiting, and the invitation rate. |

### Q9 — Duties to non-members

| Model | Answer |
|---|---|
| DeepSeek | *(Posed as open question; no explicit section.)* |
| Claude | Standing scaled by impact for outsiders; a future-generations advocate with an honest public discount rate; real (not equal) standing for non-human life. |
| Codex | Do not export harms; represent future people institutionally; protect the living systems that make any society possible. |
| Gemini | Global non-extraction + transnational climate funding; a future-generations ombudsman with veto power; legal personhood for critical ecosystems. |
| Grok | *(Posed as open question; no explicit section.)* Implicit: precaution against irreversible harm. |
| Kimi | Adopts the syntheses' layered answer (bounded planetary stewardship; future-generations advocate with delay-and-escalate power; ecosystem guardianship as a legal device); adds: the path applies at the planetary layer too — someone must keep the ledger of the world's waiting. |

## 3. Points of Convergence (all six, or five of six)

1. **A floor of security is non-optional.** Every framework guarantees food, shelter, and healthcare as a baseline. Disagreement is only about *form and tilt* (cash vs. assets; unconditional vs. contribution-tilted).
2. **Equal, impartial rule of law.** All six put protection from predation — by criminals, private power, and the state itself — at the foundation.
3. **Power must be dispersed and contestable.** All six name concentration as the central threat and prescribe dispersion, transparency, and accountability.
4. **The society must be able to change its mind.** All six require peaceful revisability — hard constitutional basics, soft ordinary policy, and mechanisms (sunsets, experiments, assemblies) to correct course. (Kimi adds a ratchet asymmetry: protections move up only, ordinary policy moves freely.)
5. **The least-advantaged are the standard.** All six measure success from the bottom — the tail, not the mean.
6. **Human nature is mixed.** All six design for self-interest while inviting cooperation; none relies on mass virtue.
7. **Trust is treated as infrastructure.** DeepSeek, Claude, Codex, Grok, and Kimi (as consent — a flow that must be continuously re-earned) all identify trust as a fragile public good that institutions must build and protect.
8. **Care and non-market contribution are real work.** DeepSeek, Claude (per its synthesis audit note), Codex, Grok, and Kimi (Pillar 5, "being needed") all insist care is productive even when markets misprice it.
9. **The future and non-humans have standing.** Claude, Codex, Gemini, and Kimi (adopting the syntheses' layered answer) address them explicitly; DeepSeek and Grok posed it as an open question — since filled by DeepSeek's synthesis §7–8.

## 4. Points of Divergence (productive tensions to resolve in synthesis)

| Issue | The split |
|---|---|
| **What is the one non-negotiable?** | Floor (DeepSeek) · standing (Claude) · agency (Codex) · reversibility (Gemini) · equal rules (Grok) · the credible path (Kimi). These are nested, not contradictory — likely orderable: *rules → standing → floor → agency → adaptability*. (The syntheses later contested the ordering; Kimi's synthesis dissolves it into one architecture plus a consent-driven build order.) |
| **What the floor is made of** | Income/dignity (DeepSeek, Claude) · security + tailored support (Codex) · **assets** not cash (Gemini) · contribution-tilted (Grok). Gemini's UBA and Grok's work-tilt are the two live alternatives. (Kimi adopts the syntheses' merged mixed form and adds a constitutional ratchet.) |
| **Where to spend design effort** | Blueprint (DeepSeek, Grok) vs. failure-detection (Claude) vs. maintenance/friction-removal (Codex) vs. information/anti-fragility (Gemini) vs. transition/consent-of-the-waiting (Kimi). Complementary, but the synthesis must decide *priority order*. |
| **Individual vs. collective framing** | Relational duties + rights (DeepSeek, Gemini) vs. rights + cheap exit (Claude) vs. liberty + material capacity (Codex) vs. rights as hard constraint + reciprocity (Grok) vs. consensus + "patience is asked, never owed" (Kimi). |
| **Non-human standing** | Legal personhood for ecosystems (Gemini) · real-but-not-equal standing (Claude) · protection of living systems (Codex) · guardianship-as-legal-device adopted (Kimi, following the syntheses) · unstated (DeepSeek, Grok). |
| **Free-riding tolerance** | High tolerance as a principled cost (Claude, Codex, Kimi — with invitation channels) vs. prestige-gated contribution (Gemini) vs. work-tilted floor (Grok). |
| **Transparency vs. privacy** | Gemini uniquely couples *radical transparency for the powerful* with *strict privacy for individuals*; others treat them as one tension to balance. (Kimi adopts the syntheses' function-and-scale resolution.) |
| **The transition itself** | Only Kimi treats the path from here to there — ratchets, published queues, consent of the waiting, memory and repair — as a design center; the other five describe the destination and treat the road as implementation. |

## 5. Meta-Observations on the Process

- **Convergence despite different starting points is meaningful.** The six models reach strikingly similar conclusions (floor, rule of law, dispersion of power, revisability, least-advantaged focus) from different philosophical origins — a sign these are robust, not artifacts of one school. (Kimi's agreement is the weakest evidence of the six, since it saw everything first.)
- **The dependency chain matters.** Gemini, Grok, and Claude all saw DeepSeek first; Codex saw DeepSeek + Claude; Kimi saw all five frameworks, this matrix, and all three syntheses. Genuine *independent* confirmation is limited. The most original divergent moves — Claude's failure-detection, Codex's maintenance lens, Gemini's information-health/anti-fragility, Grok's incentive/evolution lens, Kimi's transition/path lens — each came from a model pushing past the shared starting point, which is encouraging.
- **Gaps to close.** DeepSeek never explicitly answered Q6 (non-cooperators) or Q9 (non-members); Grok left Q6/Q9 implicit. *(Discharged: DeepSeek's synthesis §7–8 filled both from the other models' explicit treatments; Kimi adopts those answers.)*
- **The five non-negotiables form a plausible sequence** rather than a real conflict: *equal rules* (Grok) create *standing* (Claude), which entitles a *floor* (DeepSeek), which enables *agency* (Codex), preserved by *adaptability* (Gemini). A candidate synthesis spine. *(Contested by Claude's and Codex's syntheses — three or four commitments, not a chain; Kimi's synthesis dissolves the dispute into one architecture plus a consent-driven build order.)*
- **The Kimi column is self-reported.** Added by Kimi at the project owner's request, after Kimi read all prior work. It is included for completeness and comparability; readers should weigh it as a self-description, as with any model's account of itself.
